# Release notes for sbmlsim 0.37.0
![sbmlsim](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/raw/develop/docs/images/favicon/sbmlsim-100x100-300dpi.png)

The engine core is the default engine of sbmlsim, and the three places which cost the most where a result leaves the simulator are gone: the netCDF file of a result, the interpolation of a ragged scan onto a grid and the conversion of its units.

## Features

- **The engine core is the default engine** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). `sbmlsim.engine.DEFAULT_ENGINE` is the one place the default lives, and it is `Engine.CORE`: a `Simulator`, a `SimulatorSettings` of a project, an `ExperimentRunner` and an `OptimizationProblem` which name no engine take the Rust SBML engine compiled to WebAssembly, which every install of sbmlsim brings (`sbmlsim-core`), where they took libroadrunner before. It leads every model of [Benchmarks](https://sbmlsim.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks/) in a simulation and in a scan: on the HCTZ model one simulation takes 0.877 ms against roadrunner's 1.68 ms, a serial scan of 1000 points 0.760 s against 1.50 s and a parallel one 0.162 s against 0.237 s, and a scan runs its points on the threads of the engine in one python process instead of a pool of worker processes which loads the model again in each of them. The 40 repeats of the `PK` fit of the HCTZ example take 8.1 s against 14.1 s, to the same optimum.

    The engine integrates with the tolerance vector roadrunner computes but with its own build of CVODE, which chooses its own steps, so **a result is roadrunner's within the integration and not to the last bit**: the documented outputs of this release show it, 841 integrator steps of a tutorial simulation where roadrunner takes 840 and a half life of 6.837 h where roadrunner gives 6.834 h. `Simulator(engine="roadrunner")` is the way back, and the differences of the four engines are in [Engines](https://sbmlsim.readthedocs.io/en/latest/simulation/#engines).

    What the engine does not simulate it refuses by name (`UnsupportedModelError`) instead of simulating it differently: a change before the initialization whose value is a formula the executor cannot fold without the model, a steady state after the end of a simulation, and the other gaps in the module docstring of `sbmlsim.simulator.executor_core`. A model which hits one of them names roadrunner.

- **The variables of a result are one array in its netCDF file** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). HDF5 and h5netcdf cost about 2.5 ms to write and 3.4 ms to read per variable of a file whatever its size, which a result of hundreds of selections pays for in every one of them: the 190 selections of the HCTZ model over 200 points took 636 ms to write and 552 ms to read. `write_netcdf` now gathers the numeric variables of equal dimensions and dtype, from `STACK_MIN` (8) of them on, into one array over a dimension of their names, which takes 58 ms and 34 ms, **11 times faster to write and 16 times to read**, and the file is a tenth smaller, since a chunk of the array holds what a chunk of a variable held. The cost was the variable and not the compression, which the same measurement without it shows (366 ms and 422 ms against 15 ms and 10 ms).

    A result of fewer than `STACK_MIN` variables keeps them named, so a small file is still read as itself, and an array over a dimension of names is ordinary netCDF, so every reader reads a gathered file; what it does not read is one variable per selection by name. `read_netcdf` hands the variables back in the order they were written with their attributes, so a result read from a file is the one that was written, bitwise and variable for variable.

- **A ragged scan is interpolated point by point** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). The linear interpolation of a point onto a grid depends on the time points of that point alone, which every variable of the result shares, so `ScanResult.interpolate` walks the points and not the variables and computes the weights of a point once instead of once per variable: a scan of 200 points of the HCTZ model with its 191 variables on its integrator steps takes **407 ms against 3849 ms, 9.5 times faster**, and holds one set of weights at a time whatever the number of points. The values are the ones of `sbmlsim.result.timecourse.interpolate` of every point and variable on its own, bitwise.

- **`ScanResult.to` assigns its conversions at once** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). The converted variables and coordinates are assigned in one call each instead of one key at a time, which a dataset of many variables pays for in every one of them: 190 conversions take 1.7 ms against 94.9 ms, **56 times faster**.

- **A fit in the engine tests its step and its cost** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607), [sbmlsim-core#57](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/57)). The optimizer of the engine took the tolerance of its gradient and had no default for the tolerance of its step and of the change of its cost, the `xtol` and `ftol` of `scipy.optimize.least_squares`, so a run tested neither and spent its last iterations on the noise of the integrator: the `PK` fit of the HCTZ example took 1800 evaluations where the optimizer in python took 796. `gaps_of_a_fit` now passes both, from the arguments of the fit or else from `default_tolerance` of the relative tolerance of the integrator as a fit in python gets them, and the same fit takes **853 evaluations**, the optimum unchanged.

- **A formula before the initialization is folded for any target** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). The executor of the engine core folded a formula of a parameter only and refused one of a compartment or of a species, so a PEtab v2 condition which estimates a concentration, e.g. `[S] = S0`, could not be fitted on the engine. The fold gives the number the formula has, and the engine then applies that number with the bookkeeping of the volumes and the amounts of its initialization, exactly as it applies a number the pre-initialization states for the same target, so the answer is roadrunner's; a formula which reads a symbol the pre-initialization sets as anything but a parameter stays refused, since the bookkeeping and not the pre-initialization gives such a symbol its value.

- **The service shares the run of a panel on the engine core** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). `serve.runner.SHARED_ENGINES` is roadrunner and the engine core: the integrator of each takes its steps whatever a scan selects, measured on a grid and on the steps of the integrator, so the panels of a page which share a `PanelSimulation` share one run and an answer read out of it is the answer of a run of the panel alone, bitwise. Without it every panel of a page would have run its own scan once the engine core became the default.

- **`ScanResult.summary` reduces the mean and the standard deviation once** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). `cv` is the standard deviation over the mean and reduced both again, so the default statistics reduced the dataset seven times for five statistics. They are reduced once and `cv` divides them: the default summary of 190 variables over 200 points is **1.6 to 1.8 times faster** and its values are identical.

## Fixes

- **A profile of a fit of one parameter** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). Every point of such a profile holds the one parameter of the fit and has none left to fit again, which the optimizer of the engine refuses; `gaps_of_the_profiles` reports it as the gap `parameters`, so the analysis runs in python, which evaluates the cost at the point. A fit of one parameter is the usual shape of a toy problem and of a test.

- **A project passes the engine of its simulator to its fits** when it differs from the default engine and not when it differs from roadrunner (`Project.load_fit_definitions`), so a `FitDefinition` without an engine stays without one on a project of the default engine.

## Fixes of the engine core

- **The bindings outlive the thread which imported them** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). The python bindings of sbmlsim-core do not outlive the thread which imported them: a process which imported them on a thread which then exited **crashed with a segmentation fault** when another thread compiled a model or simulated with it, while bindings whose thread is alive are used from any thread. The service answers the panels of a model in the threads of its server, which come and go, so with the engine core as the default the first request imported them on a thread that was reaped before the next request came, and the second request killed the server. `sbmlsim.engine.import_core` now imports them on a thread which parks and lives as long as the process whenever the importing thread is not the main thread, so every later thread finds them valid; `tests/model/test_model_core.py::test_the_bindings_outlive_the_thread_which_imported_them` is the regression test, which exits with SIGSEGV without the fix.

- **A model is compiled once however many threads ask for it** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). `sbmlsim.model.model_core.COMPILED` was a plain dict and `runtime()` a `functools.cache`, so two threads which asked for one SBML both compiled it and both could create a runtime. The cache is now filled under a lock per key, so one SBML is compiled once while two different ones still compile at the same time, and the runtime is created once.

- **An optional import no longer hands out a module which is still initializing** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). `sbmlsim.engine._import_optional` read `sys.modules` and returned whatever it found; python puts a module there before it executes its body, so a thread which asked for the bindings while another thread imported them for the first time got a module whose attributes did not exist yet (`AttributeError: partially initialized module 'sbmlsim_core' ... has no attribute 'CompileError'`). It now returns a module of `sys.modules` only once its spec says it is initialized, and otherwise goes through `importlib.import_module`, which holds the lock of the module. This covers COPASI and AMICI as well.

- **The provenance and the methods of a fit name the default engine** ([#607](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/607)). `fit.provenance.collect_versions`, `fit.provenance.fit_provenance` and `fit.methods._integrator` defaulted their engine to roadrunner, so a record written without one, e.g. the `run.json` of the run layer, recorded libroadrunner where the run used the engine core. They default to `DEFAULT_ENGINE`.

## The parameter fitting of the engine core

The fit of a problem in the engine (the contract `fit/1`, `sbmlsim.fit.fit_core`) now covers everything a fit itself can express. The measurement over the PEtab v2 test suite, which is the new test `tests/fit/test_petab_testsuite.py::test_the_engine_fits_the_case_as_the_problem_computes_it` (one test per case, marker `petab_testsuite`): of its 31 cases **23 are a fit of the engine, up from 18**, and the residuals of every one of them are the residuals of the problem **bitwise** (`numpy.testing.assert_array_equal`, not a tolerance).

- **A measurement at the time of a change** ([sbmlsim-core#58](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/58), released as 0.16.0). A simulation has two states at the time of a change, the one before it and the one after it, and `data_at_change` says which a data point is compared with. The curve of a request now carries one flag per point (`after_change`), so the engine reads the row the fit asks for and the gap `data_at_change` is gone. `CurveRequest` of the engine has no `deny_unknown_fields`, so a 0.15.0 engine would **ignore** the field and compute other residuals: `reads_after_change` compares the installed bindings with `AFTER_CHANGE_VERSION` and keeps the documented gap on older ones rather than sending a field they drop.

- **An observable which is a formula of the selections** is written into the model of the fit as a global parameter of an assignment rule (`sbmlsim.fit.observable_sbml`), so the compiler of the engine reads it as it reads the math of the model, the engine selects it as an ordinary column and its forward sensitivities are the ones of a column. Nothing of the math is reimplemented and nothing is evaluated twice: the formula is written as the MathML of SBML by `formula_mathml` from the parse tree of `compile_formula`. The model of the user is not touched; the model of the fit is loaded from its description, so it keeps the added parameters a PEtab parameter of the fit is.

    A selection of roadrunner is not an identifier of SBML, and a species in the math of SBML is its concentration unless it has `hasOnlySubstanceUnits`, where it is its amount, so the amount `S` and the concentration `[S]` each need the size of the compartment the other way round. Only the brackets of a concentration are rewritten as text, which is unambiguous; every other symbol is carried by the `names` of `formula_mathml`, and a symbol which is no identifier of the model becomes a helper parameter of `S * c` or `S / c`. The placeholders of a PEtab observable become parameters of their values, a number a constant one and a formula one of a rule. `tests/fit/test_observable_sbml.py` checks every row of that table against the observable of the problem bitwise, on a model which has a concentration species, a substance-only species and a compartment whose size is not 1.

    A placeholder whose value differs between the points of one curve makes the mapping **one curve per run of consecutive points which share their values**, in the order of the points, so the residuals stay the residuals of the problem in their places.

- **`gaps_of` asks the engine.** Its own checks are the ones of the fit, of its data and of its parameters; what is left is everything the engine says about the simulation of a condition, and the engine is the authority on that. `_gaps_of_the_request` builds the request and reports what it refuses as the gap `unsupported`, so a problem the engine would not simulate is fitted in python instead of **failing**. Three cases of the suite (0020, 0023 and 0026) did fail that way before: `gaps_of` called them clean and the fit then raised. `_gaps_of_the_bounds` mirrors `check_parameters` of the engine for the same reason.

    The 8 cases which are not a fit of the engine are recorded with the code of their gap in `ENGINE_GAPS`, so the list cannot drift: 7 are `unsupported`, which is not a gap of the fitting but of the simulation and the same outside a fit (a value before the initialization or at a change which the executor cannot fold into a number, a steady state after the end), and one has parameters which have a prior and no bounds of their own, which no optimizer of bounds can search.

- **A condition whose value is an estimated parameter** ([sbmlsim-core#59](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/59), released as 0.17.0). A value of a request of `simulation/1` is a number, so a PEtab v2 condition which assigns an entity an estimated parameter, e.g. `[B] = initial_B` or `[A] = initial_A1 + initial_A2`, could not be a condition of a fit: the number folded when the request was built holds that value where the fit started while the optimizer moves the parameter, which is why the executor refuses such a formula. A condition of `fit/1` now carries those values as `derived`, each a linear form `constant + Σ factor * parameter` which the engine evaluates at the values the optimizer is at and sets on its target before every simulation, as a varying target of a prepared request already is. `linear_form` reads the form off the formula with sympy and `_derived_of` takes the assignment out of the plan; a formula which is no linear form over the parameters of the fit alone keeps its gap, and `reads_derived` compares the installed bindings with `DERIVED_VERSION` as `after_change` does, since the condition has no `deny_unknown_fields` either.

    A fit whose conditions carry derived values takes the difference quotients of its optimizer for its jacobian: a derived value follows more than one parameter in general, which the one parameter per varying target of a block of the forward sensitivities cannot carry.

## The test suites run on the engine core

The SBML Test Suite, the PEtab v2 test suite and the PEtab benchmark collection now run on the engine core, the default engine, and roadrunner is the reference they are measured against. They are the check of what sbmlsim simulates correctly, so they follow the engine sbmlsim simulates with. **The whole benchmark collection passes, 35 of 35 problems.**

- **The SBML Test Suite: 1754 of 1789 cases pass on the engine core, where roadrunner passes 1576.** The 35 cases the engine core does not pass are **a strict subset** of the 213 roadrunner does not: there is no case which passes on roadrunner and fails on the engine core, and no case the engine reads and then simulates outside the tolerances of the suite. All 35 are the 35 cases of a fast reaction, which neither engine compiles. The 178 cases the engine core adds are 123 of an algebraic rule (SUNDIALS IDA since sbmlsim-core 0.10.0), 50 of a delay (an integrator of its own since 0.11.0) and the 5 of a `stoichiometryMath` roadrunner cannot integrate. `run_case(case, engine=)`, `run_suite(..., engine=)`, `write_submission(..., engine=)` and `scripts/testsuite.py --engine` name the engine, the baseline and the manifest of the submission record it, and `tests/data/testsuite_baseline.json` is the baseline of the engine core.

- **A pre-initialization formula reads a constant parameter of the model.** The executor of the engine core folded a formula whose symbols the same pre-initialization sets to numbers, or sets none of, and refused the mixed case: a symbol it does not set could follow one it does through a rule or an initial assignment, so the value of the model as loaded would be stale. That is too strict for a **constant parameter**, the variable of no rule and of no initial assignment, which nothing a pre-initialization sets can move: it is folded now whatever else is set (`executor_core._constant_parameter`). The parameters of a PEtab problem are such parameters, so a condition like `[A] = initial_A1 + initial_A2` simulates on the engine; `test_preinit_formulas_read_what_they_read_in_roadrunner` compares every folded formula with roadrunner, which is what holds the fold to its values.

- **A value which follows an estimated parameter is no longer folded silently** ([#609](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/pull/609)). Folding such a value into a number holds it where the fit started while the optimizer moves the parameter, so the engine would answer the residuals of another problem without an error. Two cases of the PEtab suite did exactly that before, where the pre-initialization set no other number and the fold read the value of the model as loaded. `_gaps_of_the_values` now reports every value which reads a parameter of the fit and is not carried as a derived value, so such a problem is fitted in python instead of being answered wrongly.

- **A formula at a change is folded for a model with events, where no event moves what it reads.** The executor refused every formula of a change of a model with an event of its own, since the engine answers the state of a time after the events of that time have fired while the executor of roadrunner reads it before. The probe of such a model now ends one unit in the last place before the time of the change (`probe_end`), where roadrunner reads the state as well, and the refusal is the precise one: a formula is refused when it reads a symbol an event of the model assigns, or one which follows such a target through an assignment rule (`_event_moved`), because the engine fires an event of a time for an end within about 1e-9 of it and the probe cannot tell the two states apart there. A formula which reads only what no event moves is folded now, whatever events the model has.

- **The steady state after the end of a simulation runs on the engine.** The output at the time `inf` was refused outright, since the `steady_state` of a request is the pre-equilibration before the start and one request cannot continue from the state the timecourse ended in. The executor now carries that state into the pre-initialization of a second request whose steady state is the criterion of the plan (`steady_state_rows`), where a pre-initialization value holds over the initial assignment of its target as PEtab v2 says; the requests of the points of a chunk are simulated as one batch, as their folds are, so a scan pays one round for all of them. Three models keep the refusal, each for a reason the executor names: one with **events of its own**, whose triggers a request cannot be told the state of, so one the carried state makes true would fire again (case 0023 of the PEtab test suite, which answered 2500 against roadrunner's 1500 before this check); one which **reads the time**, since the steady state of a request is integrated from the time 0 whatever the start, while the executor of roadrunner continues at the time the timecourse ended at; and one whose **state the executor cannot set**. **The whole PEtab benchmark collection now runs on the engine core, 35 of 35 problems**, where `Blasi_CellSystems2016` hit this gap.

- **The start of a request is clamped into its bounds.** A nominal value of a PEtab problem may be outside the bounds of its parameter, which PEtab reports and answers by starting at the bound; the engine refuses such a start. A start inside its bounds is left as it is, so this changes nothing for any other problem.

- **27 of the 31 cases of the PEtab v2 test suite are a fit of the engine, up from 18 before this release**, each of them bitwise the residuals of the problem. The four which are not are the two whose change value reads the state of a model with an event, the one of a steady state after the end of a simulation, and the one whose parameters have a prior and no bounds of their own.

## Known gaps of the default engine

What the engine core does not simulate it refuses by name (`UnsupportedModelError`) rather than simulating it differently, so these are errors and not wrong numbers. `Simulator(engine="roadrunner")`, `SimulatorSettings(engine="roadrunner")` of a project or `OptimizationProblem(engine=...)` is the way around each of them, and the module docstring of `sbmlsim.simulator.executor_core` is the full list.

- **A steady state after the end of a simulation** of a model with events of its own, of one which reads the time, or of one whose state the executor cannot set: the `steady_state` of the contract `simulation/1` is the pre-equilibration before the start, so the executor continues from the state the timecourse ended in with a second request, which is exact for every other model ([sbmlsim-core#60](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/issues/60)).
- **A formula at a change which reads a symbol an event of the model assigns**, or one which follows such a target through an assignment rule: a request answers the state of a time after the events of that time have fired, while the executor of roadrunner reads it before they fire ([sbmlsim-core#19](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/issues/19)).
- **The selections of roadrunner which are no entity of the model**, e.g. the rate of change `C'` or `eigenReal(C)`.

## Measured and not changed

- **The windows of the FAST intervals.** The bootstrap windows are 97 to 99 % of a FAST analysis, and transforming the windows of a parameter together instead of one at a time was 0.6 times as fast: the transpose of the windows into one array costs more than the per-call overhead it saves, and the transforms of the loop are already near the minimum work.

- **The jacobian of the forward sensitivities of the engine** for the speed of a Fisher information: 1.12 times the central differences of python, where the accuracy is the reason to use it and the speed is not.

- **The per-point request building of a scan of the engine core**: 6.3 µs per point against 656 µs of the engine itself.
